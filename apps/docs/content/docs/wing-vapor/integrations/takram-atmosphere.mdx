Vapor is white because it scatters, so under
[`@takram/three-atmosphere`](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@takram/three-atmosphere)
it should scatter the atmosphere's own light. Bring the sun down: the
sunlight through more air reddens and dims, the sky goes from blue to dusk,
and the vapor follows both, at the exposure the sky meters.

The setup, the volume pass with the aerial perspective as its backdrop and
the scene's place on the earth, is on [core's page](/docs/core/integrations/takram-atmosphere).

## The light at the aircraft

The vapor's look takes the sun as an irradiance, scattered through the
droplets' phase function, and the sky as a radiance, the same from every
direction. Takram's `getSplitScalarIlluminance(point, sunDirection)` gives
both at a point from the tables the sky is drawn with; a one-thread compute
pass reads them back at the aircraft whenever the sun or the altitude move.

- **The sun** is its `direct`: the solar irradiance through the air above,
  in the atmosphere's units. Its colour is the sunset.
- **The sky** is its `indirect` / 2π, E<sub>h</sub>, the dome's horizontal
  illuminance. As a radiance the dome is E<sub>h</sub> / π, and the ground
  below, lit by sun and sky, is albedo × (E<sub>sun</sub> sin e + E<sub>h</sub>) / π,
  with takram's ground albedo of 0.3. The vapor's single sky is the mean of
  the two.

```ts
<WingVapor
  capture={scene}
  look={{
    sunDirection, // towards the sun, world space
    sunColor: lighting.sun,
    sunIntensity: 1,
    skyColor: lighting.sky,
    skyIntensity: 1,
  }}
/>
```

Both are already in the atmosphere's units, so the look's own `exposure`
stays at 1 and the camera photographs the vapor with the sky.

The vapor gets no aerial perspective of its own: within a few hundred metres
the air in front of it takes about 1 % of its light.
