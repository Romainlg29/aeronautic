<Example name="weather" />

The climates under
[`@takram/three-atmosphere`](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@takram/three-atmosphere)'s
sky. Bring the sun down and the clouds redden with the light that reaches
them through the long air, and their shaded sides darken.

The setup, the volume pass with the aerial perspective as its backdrop and
the scene's place on the earth, is on [core's page](/docs/core/integrations/takram-atmosphere).

## In the backdrop

The clouds go in front of the atmosphere's aerial perspective, and the
volume pass's plumes and vapour over both. So they are composited at the end
of the backdrop:

```tsx
const clouds = useClouds();

const split = useMemo(
  () =>
    volume_pass(scene, camera, {
      backdropNode: ({ color, depth }) => {
        const aired = aerialPerspective(color, depth);

        return clouds ? clouds.over(aired, depth) : aired;
      },
    }),
  [scene, camera, clouds],
);
```

`over` takes the scene's depth: the clouds stop at the airframe, and the
terrain hides those behind it.

## The light

The look is the atmosphere's light where the scene is, read with takram's
`getSplitScalarIlluminance` (see core's page), all of it already in the
atmosphere's units, so the exposure is 1:

```ts
const sin_elevation = Math.max(Math.sin(elevation), 0);

weather.set({
  look: {
    sunDirection: sun_world.toArray(),
    // The solar irradiance through the air above
    sun: direct,
    // The dome's horizontal irradiance, as a Lambertian radiance
    sky: indirect.map((e) => e / (2 * Math.PI) / Math.PI),
    // The sunlit ground under it, of albedo GROUND_ALBEDO
    ground: direct.map(
      (e, i) =>
        (GROUND_ALBEDO * (e * sin_elevation + indirect[i] / (2 * Math.PI))) /
        Math.PI,
    ),
    exposure: 1,
    visibilityM: AIR_VISIBILITY_M,
    // The sky along the horizon, averaged all the way round
    haze: horizon,
  },
});
```

The sun's light reaches the clouds already reddened by the air between them
and space. The air between the camera and the clouds is takram's own, as
`visibilityM`: Koschmieder's 3.912 over its extinction at 550 nm. The haze
it fades to is the horizon's radiance, read with takram's
`getIndirectLuminance` along eight bearings round it, so far clouds fade
into the bright horizon sky, not into a darker dome.

Takram's sky stops at the air in front of the ground, so from a few hundred
metres up everything under the horizon is near black. The example lights
the ground where the backdrop's ray meets it: its albedo under the sun and
the sky there, over π, through the air between. The ray is the scene
camera's, with its own matrices: inside a pass, `cameraWorldMatrix` is the
pass's quad camera.
