The weather's frame is metres, x and z along the ground and y up. The
scene's origin sits in it at `origin`: x, an altitude, and z. The clouds are
drawn and sampled round it.

## Moving through it

Give the weather a velocity and its clock moves the scene through it, as an
aircraft flies through the sky while the scene stays round it:

```tsx
const weather = useWeather();

useFrame(() => {
  if (weather) weather.velocity = [vx, vz]; // metres a second
});
```

Or set `<Weather origin={[x, z]}>` where the scene is. Its altitude is the
`<FlightProvider>`'s flight's, or `altitudeM` outside one. The clouds drift
with the climate's winds and grow and die as the clock runs;
`timeScale={0}` holds them still.

## In the flight's air

Inside a `<FlightProvider>`, `<Weather>` makes the flight's air its own each
frame: its temperature the climate's, and its relative humidity 1 while the
aircraft is in cloud, where the air is saturated, and its own again outside.
The vapour and the contrails, which read the flight's air, form as they
would in the cloud. `aircraft` is a ref to the aircraft when it is not at
the scene's origin; `couple={false}` leaves the flight alone.

## The effects inside the cloud

Drawn alone, the clouds would go behind every effect in front of the camera,
whole however much cloud lies between. So inside a cloud `useClouds` sets
the cloud round the camera as the scene's medium, and the plumes, the
vapour, the contrails and the countermeasures fade into it with how far they
are, lit by the light that gets into the cloud. `scene: null` turns it off.

## Sampling it

`weather.sample(x, y, z)` is the air at a point of the scene, on the CPU,
from the same seed and the same functions the GPU draws:

```ts
const air = weather.sample(0, 0, 0);

air.inCloud; // droplets or ice
air.extinction; // per metre: water, ice and rain together
air.visibilityM; // Koschmieder's range in it
air.rain; // the rain's share of the extinction, per metre
```

What an aircraft flies into is what is drawn.
