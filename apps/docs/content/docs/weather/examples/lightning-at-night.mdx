At night a meter alone would open the camera up until the empty sky was
grey, and the dusk's last colour with it. The example holds it at a
low-light video camera's limit instead, `lowestEv100` on the stage: f/1.4,
1/30 s, ISO 12 800, an estimate of a good one, EV<sub>100</sub> −1.1. The
sky stays black, the faint stars under it, and the storm hides those behind
it, so what the flashes light is all there is to see.

Bring the storm in to 4 km and the strokes come down in front of you, under
its base, through the edge of its rain. Nearer, the camera would look into
the rain's lit wall half a kilometre off, and the strokes would fall to
either side of the frame.

Its rain falls downshear, beyond the tower, and the base on this side is
rain-free: under the updraft hangs the wall cloud, lit from below by the
strokes that come down past it.
