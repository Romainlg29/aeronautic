<Example name="contrails" />

The fighter cruises at Mach 0.85, and its trails are laid in the air it has
flown through: twenty seconds of them, five kilometres. Nothing about them is
drawn to a shape or a timer.

- **The gap behind the nozzles** is the 0.14 s the exhaust takes to cool
  enough by mixing to pass water saturation: 36 m at this speed.
- **Two trails become one** as the vortices off each wingtip draw them in and
  the pair sinks with the wake, some 90 m before the vortices link up and
  break, half a minute behind.
- **They thin as they widen**: the same ice spread over more air.
- **Pull the humidity under 100 %** over ice and the trails evaporate a few
  seconds behind the aircraft. Over it, they persist.
- **Lower the altitude** and the air warms past the threshold: at 10 km on a
  standard day, the fighter trails nothing.
- **Switch to hydrogen** and the trails form further behind, in warmer air,
  and are fainter: fewer, larger crystals.
- **Turn** and the trails curve behind the aircraft along the path it flew.
  At 2 g the wake is twice as strong and sinks twice as fast, and links up
  in half the time.

The line under the controls says what the physics made of the day.
