The in-the-sun example under
[`@takram/three-atmosphere`](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@takram/three-atmosphere)'s
sky. Hold the chaff and it streams out behind the fighter, lit by the
sunlight and skylight the atmosphere works out three kilometres up. Bring the
sun down below the horizon and fire the flares: the camera opens up for the
dimmer sky, and the same flames come up brighter against it.

The setup, the volume pass with the aerial perspective as its backdrop and
the scene's place on the earth, is on [core's page](/docs/core/integrations/takram-atmosphere).

## In the volume pass

The atmosphere's aerial perspective draws the sky wherever the opaque scene
left nothing, so whatever is drawn over the sky in the scene itself is drawn
over again. `<Countermeasures>` finds the volume pass through its
`<VolumePassContext>`, as the plumes, the vapor and the contrails do, and
draws its flames, smoke, chaff and glare in it, over the atmosphere's
backdrop. The point lights stay in the aircraft, lighting the scene.

The pass has no depth of its own: each of them reads the opaque scene's from
`pass.backdrop` instead, so the airframe still hides what is behind it.
Without React, give `backdrop` to the `Countermeasures` and put its `glare`,
`trail`, `smoke` and `clouds` in `pass.scene`.

## The light units

The look's `exposure` is the scene's value for 1 cd/m². The atmosphere draws
one unit on screen as about 75 700 cd/m², so under it the exposure is its
`luminanceScale`, whatever the camera meters:

```ts
import { AtmosphereParameters } from "@takram/three-atmosphere/webgpu";

const exposure = new AtmosphereParameters().luminanceScale; // ≈ 1.3 × 10⁻⁵

<Countermeasures
  look={{
    exposure,
    sunDirection, // towards the sun, world space
    sunColor: lighting.sun,
    sunIntensity: 1,
    skyColor: lighting.sky,
    skyIntensity: 1,
  }}
/>
```

The sun and the sky are taken as the contrails take them, from takram's
`getSplitScalarIlluminance` at the aircraft:

- **The sun** is its `direct`: the solar irradiance through the air above.
  It lights the smoke through its phase function, and the chaff by its
  glints and its flash at the sun's reflection.
- **The sky** is the mean of the dome above, E<sub>h</sub> / π from its
  horizontal illuminance, and the sunlit ground below: the light that
  falls on the smoke and the chaff from every side.

Both are already in the atmosphere's units, so their intensities stay at 1.
The flames are in candelas, the exposure turning them into the same units.

## The air in front of it

The pass's contents get no aerial perspective from the atmosphere. The
countermeasures put their own haze in front of them, from the `air`'s
visibility: at its default 23 km, Koschmieder's 3.912 / V is 0.17 per
kilometre, and the example's few hundred metres keep over 95 % of the
light. Takram's clear air three kilometres up, about 0.01 per kilometre in
green, is nearly twenty times thinner;
give `visibilityM` to match it if the scene is seen from far off.

The glitter is counted pixel by pixel, in the pass's pixels: at its half
resolution it is coarser, with the same mean.
