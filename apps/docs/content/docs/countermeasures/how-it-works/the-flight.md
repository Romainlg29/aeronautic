---
title: The flight
description: "Thrown, slowed by its drag, falling."
---

The flare leaves the dispenser at the cartridge's ejection speed, 30.3 m/s
(US patent 5,400,712 measured 99.5 ft/s), on top of the aircraft's velocity
through the air. From then on two forces act on it: its drag and its weight.

It is a small dense block, tumbling. A tumbling convex body shows, on
average, a quarter of its surface to the flow (Cauchy's formula), and a
block's drag coefficient is a cube's, about 1.05 (Hoerner, Fluid-Dynamic
Drag, 1965). At 250 m/s near sea level that slows it at about 770 m/s²,
nearly 80 g: it is far behind the aircraft within a second. Once slowed, it
falls at the 28 m/s its drag allows at sea level, a little more higher up.
As it burns, it gets lighter for its size, and slows faster.

The flares are laid in the air, not in the aircraft's frame: each moves at
its own velocity less the aircraft's, so a turning aircraft leaves them where
they were let go. The air's density is the standard atmosphere's at the
altitude, or the shared flight's.

The steps are at most 5 ms, the drag taken implicitly, so a long frame or a
slow tab flies them the same.
