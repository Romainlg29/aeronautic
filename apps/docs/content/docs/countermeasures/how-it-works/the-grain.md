---
title: The grain
description: "A block of magnesium, Teflon and Viton, burning from every face."
---

An MJU-7 class flare is a 1 × 2 × 8 inch cartridge, the one most western
fighters carry. Chemring publishes its drop-in, the CM 218, as 253 g of MTV in
a 51.8 × 24.6 mm case. The grain fills the case less its walls, a millimetre
each side.

MTV1, the baseline composition, is half magnesium, 45 % Teflon and 5 % Viton
by mass. Pressed to its theoretical density, the mass over the sum of each
ingredient's volume, it is 1906 kg/m³, so 253 g is a grain about 12 cm long.

It burns from every face at once, each receding at the composition's linear
burn rate: 3.86 mm/s for MTV1, as Debnath et al. measure it (CEJEM 22(2),
2025). A block a × b × c is (a − 2rt) × (b − 2rt) × (c − 2rt) at t, and is
gone when its thinnest side is: 22.6 mm over twice 3.86 mm/s, 2.9 s.

What it burns a second is its density times the rate times its burning
surface. It burns fastest as it lights, and less as it shrinks.

## Left out

- **The burn rate's pressure**: an MTV grain burns a little slower in thin
  air. It is the measured rate at every altitude.
- **Ignition**: the grain lights as it leaves the dispenser, where a real
  one's igniter takes a fraction of a second.
