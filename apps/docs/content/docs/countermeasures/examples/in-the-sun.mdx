<Example name="chaff-in-the-sun" />

The fighter flies at 250 m/s, three kilometres up on a clear day. Hold the
button and the dispensers take turns, a cartridge every twentieth of a
second (a fast program, an **estimate**): a cloud every 12.5 m, about its
own length, so the trail streams out behind it. Nothing about its light is
drawn to taste: the sun's illuminance through the air (Meinel and Meinel's
fit, Kasten and Young's air mass), a clear sky's 6000 cd/m², the ground's
fifth of what falls on it, and the aluminium's 0.91.

- **Each cloud is 11 m long** at 244 m/s, stretched by the airspeed as it
  blooms, and 1.75 m across. Slow down and they shorten; hover and they hang
  under the tail.
- **The sun sets how it shines.** Level fibres are mirrors facing up: from
  where the sun's reflection would be, the cloud flashes about eighteen
  times brighter than turned every way, within a few degrees. The sun
  starts behind the trail as high as the first view sees it, so the trail
  shines white.
- **Move the sun** and the flash leaves: away from the reflection the same
  cloud is only grey against the sky. Its fibres hide about a fifth of
  what is behind it, as thin as real chaff.
- **Above, behind the trail**, put the sun ahead of the nose, about 25° up,
  and the trail flashes towards you.
- **Chasing**, the clouds go past the camera in a tenth of a second: at 250
  m/s the trail is left behind fast.
- **Where it is thin it glitters**: each pixel draws its own count of
  glinting fibres, and the count changes as the view turns.
- **It sinks** at the readout's speed and stays where it was let go: the
  fighter is the one that leaves.
