<Example name="chaff-in-the-sun" />

The fighter hovers a kilometre up on a clear day, the sun ahead of it. Let
the chaff go and each cartridge's cloud forms under the tail. Nothing about
its light is drawn to taste: the sun's illuminance through the air
(Meinel and Meinel's fit, Kasten and Young's air mass), a clear sky's 6000
cd/m², and the aluminium's 0.91.

- **At the sun's reflection**, behind the tail as high as the sun, the
  level fibres face you as mirrors: the cloud flashes, about eighteen times
  brighter than turned every way, within a few degrees.
- **Off to the side**, the same cloud is lit less than turned every way: a
  level mirror sends the sun's light into the reflection, not this way.
- **Below, into the sun**, every fibre's cone reaches the eye.
- **Orbit the camera** and the readout follows how bright the cloud is from
  there, against fibres turned every way.
- **The first fifth of a second** the fibres are still tumbling out of the
  airstream: the cloud is lit every way alike until they level.
- **Where it is thin it glitters**: each pixel draws its own count of
  glinting fibres.
- **Raise the sun** and its reflection climbs with it, the view following.
