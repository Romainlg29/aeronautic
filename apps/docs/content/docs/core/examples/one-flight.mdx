<Example name="one-flight" />

Everything here reads one `Flight`. It is made outside the canvas and handed to
two providers: one round the page's overlay, where the sliders write it and the
readout reads it with `useFlight`, and one inside the canvas, round the
fighter. React's context does not cross into a `<Canvas>`, so the same flight is
given to both.

- **Mach** writes the airspeed, worked out from the speed of sound in the air
  the flight is in now. The vapor reads it.
- **Angle of attack** writes the angle, and holds the stick back with it so
  the elevons show it. The vapor, the leading-edge flaps and the elevons read
  it, and the fighter is pitched up by it so the flight path stays level.
- **Throttle** opens and closes the nozzles' petals, moves the throttle lever
  in the cockpit and lights the plumes, each on its engine's exhaust.
- **Altitude** changes the air: its temperature, its pressure and how far it
  is from its dew point. The plumes and the vapor are drawn in that air, and
  the readout follows it.

The readout is a `useFlight` per number, at 10 Hz for the ones that move, so
the overlay renders a few times a second however fast the scene draws.

The fighter carries `<ControlSurfaces exhaust={<Exhaust />} />`, which puts an
`<Engine>` on each of its nozzles and the plume inside each. See
[the controls](../../../controls/examples/the-controls/) for every part on
its own slider.
