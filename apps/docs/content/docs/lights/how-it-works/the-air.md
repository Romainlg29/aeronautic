---
title: The air
description: "Haze dimming and reddening the lights."
---

Between a light and the eye the air takes some of its light: the molecules,
far more of the blue than the red, and the haze, a little more of the blue.

- **The molecules**: Rayleigh scattering, 5.8, 13.6 and 33.1 per million
  metres at 680, 550 and 440 nm at sea level, thinning with the air's
  density.
- **The haze**: what the day's visibility leaves. Koschmieder's law gives the
  extinction at 550 nm as 3.912 over the visibility, the distance at which a
  black object's contrast falls to 2 %. What the molecules do not account
  for is the haze, carried to the other wavelengths with an Ångström
  exponent of 1.3.

A light at the visibility keeps 2 % of its green, and less of its blue: far
lights redden. The beams' light in the air is that same scattering seen from
the side. The haze scatters mostly forward, with an asymmetry of 0.7, and
absorbs a tenth of what it meets.
